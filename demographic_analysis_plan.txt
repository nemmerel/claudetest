Plan for Capital Gains Demographic Analysis
============================================

This plan responds to Moll's request for "careful empirical documentation of the
distribution of capital gains" and how they "correlate with various income sources
and demographics." Available demographic variables: age, geographic location, gender.


1. AGE ANALYSIS
---------------

Core Statistics to Generate:
- Mean and median capital gains by 5-year age brackets (20-24, 25-29, ... 80+)
- Capital gains concentration by age decile (what share of total gains goes to each age group)
- Capital gains per capita by age group (controlling for population size)
- Breakdown by asset class: For each age group, show composition (equities, housing, pensions, private business)

Life-Cycle Patterns:
- Age-gains profiles conditional on income percentile (do high-income young people have different gain patterns than high-income elderly?)
- Cohort analysis: track birth cohorts over time to see if the relationship between age and gains is changing
- Ratio of capital gains to labor income by age (shows shift from wages to asset appreciation over lifecycle)

Time Trends:
- How has the age distribution of capital gains changed 2002-2021?
- Has concentration in elderly increased over time as noted in revised text?


2. GEOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS
----------------------

State-Level:
- Total capital gains by state, both levels and per capita
- Capital gains as share of state income
- Ranking of states by capital gains concentration
- Decomposition: which asset classes drive state-level variation (California = tech equity vs. Texas = real estate/energy?)

Finer Geographic Detail (if available):
- MSA-level analysis for major metros
- Urban vs. rural decomposition
- Correlation with local housing price appreciation, presence of corporate headquarters, financial sector employment

Regional Patterns:
- Regional breakdown (Northeast, South, Midwest, West)
- Coastal vs. interior
- Correlation with income levels, educational attainment, industry composition


3. GENDER ANALYSIS
------------------

Basic Statistics:
- Mean/median capital gains by gender
- Share of total capital gains received by men vs. women
- Gender gap in capital gains by income percentile (is the gap larger at the top?)

Asset Class Decomposition:
- Do men and women have different asset class compositions of gains?
- Housing gains vs. equity gains by gender

Conditional Analysis:
- Gender gap controlling for age and income
- Gender gap among tax filers vs. accounting for joint filers


4. CROSS-DEMOGRAPHIC ANALYSIS
-----------------------------

Intersectional Tables:
- Age x Gender: Capital gains by age-gender cells
- Age x Geography: Are elderly gains more concentrated in certain states (Florida, Arizona)?
- Income x Demographics: How do demographic patterns vary across the income distribution?

Correlations:
- Present correlation matrix: capital gains with labor income, capital income, wealth, realized gains, age, etc.
- Regression decomposition: What share of variation in capital gains is explained by age vs. income vs. geography?


5. SUGGESTED FIGURES AND TABLES
-------------------------------

For Age Section:
- Figure: Bar chart of mean capital gains by age group with asset class stacking
- Figure: Line graph showing share of total gains by age group over time (2002-2021)
- Table: Capital gains statistics by age decile (mean, median, share of total, N)

For Geography Section:
- Figure: Choropleth map of per-capita capital gains by state
- Table: Top 10 and bottom 10 states by capital gains measures
- Figure: Scatter plot of state capital gains vs. income levels

For Gender Section:
- Table: Capital gains by gender, overall and by income quintile
- Figure: Gender gap in capital gains by asset class

Summary Table:
- Comprehensive demographic breakdown showing means, medians, and concentration measures across all three dimensions

7. RESPONDING TO MOLL'S FRAMING
-------------------------------

This analysis directly addresses Moll's request by:
1. Documenting "key facts" about capital gains distribution
2. Showing "how capital gains correlate with various income sources and demographics"
3. Avoiding Haig-Simons income calculations or tax rate implications in the demographic sections
4. Presenting descriptive facts rather than normative claims about income measurement

The demographic analysis can stand as its own findings section focused purely on
"who receives capital gains" rather than "what this implies for income inequality
measurement."
